Topics

Learn

Learn these six words first. Every later station uses them.

Outstanding deposit: a deposit recorded on the check stub but not yet on the bank statement.

Outstanding check: a check that was written and recorded but has not yet been paid by the bank.

Dishonored check: a check the bank refuses to pay, for example because the writer's account does not have enough money (NSF).

Electronic funds transfer (EFT): paying cash by computer, with no check or paper.

Debit card: a bank card that takes the amount of a purchase straight out of the checking account.

Petty cash: cash kept on hand for making small payments.

Tips & tricks

  • Outstanding = still out there. An outstanding deposit or check is one you recorded that the bank has not shown yet.
  • Deposit goes in, check goes out. Outstanding deposit = money coming in. Outstanding check = money going out.
  • NSF stands for Not Sufficient Funds. A dishonored check is a bounced check.
  • EFT has no paper at all: no check, no cash. The money just moves by computer.
  • Debit card takes the money out of your checking account right away. It is not a loan like a credit card.

An endorsement is the signature and instructions on the back of a check that transfer or deposit it. There are three kinds. The business is Lakeshore Bike Shop, owned by Tom Alvarez.

TypeWhat you writeSafety
BlankSignature onlyLowest
SpecialPay to the order of [new owner] + signatureMedium
RestrictiveFor deposit only to the account of [business] + signatureHighest

Tips & tricks

  • Blank = bare. Just a signature. Anyone who finds the check could cash it, so it is the riskiest.
  • Special = Specific person. It names who gets the money: Pay to the order of a name.
  • Restrictive = Restricted to the bank. For deposit only means the check can only be deposited. It is the safest.
  • Write the endorsement on the back, above the line that says DO NOT WRITE, STAMP, OR SIGN BELOW THIS LINE.
  • Always finish with the signature. The signature is the same name as on the front: the person or business the check was written to.

Learn

The check stub is your own record of every check and deposit. It keeps a running balance. Here is a worked example for Lakeshore Bike Shop.

Balance brought forward ......... 1,500.00 Amount deposited ................. + 300.00 Subtotal ......................... 1,800.00 Amount this check ................ − 250.00 Balance carried forward .......... 1,550.00

Put the eight stub steps in order. Two of them involve math; do them in your head or on paper as you go.

Tips & tricks

  • Chant: Amount, Date, To, For, Deposit, Subtotal, This check, Balance.
  • Add before you subtract. Subtotal = balance brought forward + deposit. Balance carried forward = subtotal − this check.
  • Check your answer: the new balance should be smaller than the subtotal, unless there was no check.
  • Fill the stub first, then write the check. They must show the same amount, date, payee and purpose.
  • The OTHER line is for things the bank charges you or adds, like a service charge. Subtract it right there, then write a second subtotal.
  • Money in the stub is always written with cents. 300 becomes 300.00.

Learn

The words on a check must match the numbers. Use this frame every time:

[dollars in words] and [cents]/100 ———————— DOLLARS

Match each amount to the way it is correctly written on the check. Three of the six responses are traps.

Tips & tricks

  • Dollars in words, cents as a fraction. $145.60 becomes One hundred forty-five and 60/100.
  • Only one “and.” Use it once, right before the cents. Never write “one hundred and forty-five.”
  • Cents are always over 100: 5 cents is 05/100, not 5/100. Even 00 cents is written 00/100.
  • Use a hyphen for 21 to 99 when two words are joined: twenty-five, forty-five.
  • Finish with a long line out to the word DOLLARS so nobody can add words after yours.
  • Mix-up warning: sixty-three and thirty-six look alike. Read the number slowly.

Learn

A bank reconciliation compares the bank statement to your check stubs and explains any difference. The goal: the adjusted bank balance and the adjusted check stub balance must be equal.

Sort each item for Lakeshore Bike Shop. Four bins: two change the bank balance, one changes the stub balance, and one is not used at all.

Tips & tricks

  • Bank side: things the bank doesn't know about yet. Outstanding deposits are added, outstanding checks are subtracted.
  • Stub side: things your stub doesn't know about yet. A bank service charge is subtracted.
  • VOID checks never matter. A voided check was never sent, so it isn't outstanding and it isn't subtracted.
  • A check that already appears on the statement is cleared. It is not outstanding.
  • Ask: Who has not heard about it yet? The bank? Fix the bank side. Me? Fix my stub.

Learn

Adjusted bank balance = Bank statement balance + Outstanding deposits − Outstanding checks Adjusted stub balance = Latest stub balance − Service charge

Lakeshore Bike Shop facts: bank statement balance 3,100.00. Outstanding deposit 400.00. Outstanding checks 220.00 and 75.00. Service charge 12.00. Latest check stub balance 3,217.00. Check No. 222 was VOID.

Tips & tricks

  • Bank side: statement balance + outstanding deposits − outstanding checks.
  • Stub side: latest stub balance − service charge.
  • Add the outstanding checks together first, then subtract the total once. It cuts down on mistakes.
  • The two adjusted balances MUST match. If they don't, check your addition, then look for a missing check or charge.
  • Use the latest check stub balance, the one from the last stub written, not the first.

Learn

Each transaction below debits one account. Match each transaction to the account that is debited. Cash is credited in all four, so it is a trap here.

Tips & tricks

  • Cash is the credit in almost every entry in this chapter. Money leaves the checking account, so Cash is credited.
  • Always ask: what did I get or what did I use up? You get supplies: debit Supplies. The bank takes a fee: debit Miscellaneous Expense.
  • Document numbers: C means a check you wrote (C + check number). M means a memorandum, used for debit card payments and bank-originated items like a service charge or a dishonored check.
  • Setting up petty cash debits Petty Cash because the fund is now an asset, and credits Cash.
  • Dishonored check: the customer owes you again, so debit that customer's Accounts Receivable.

Learn

A dishonored check comes back unpaid. Your bank takes the money out of your account again and may charge a fee. Example: Priya Shah gave Lakeshore Bike Shop a 180.00 check. It bounced and the bank charged a 30.00 fee.

Debit Accounts Receivable—Priya Shah   210.00Credit Cash   210.00

Tips & tricks

  • Dishonored check amount = the check + the bank fee. The customer owes both again.
  • The credit side is Cash because the bank took the money back out of your account.
  • The debit is to Accounts Receivable for that customer, not to Sales and not to an expense.
  • C vs. M: if you wrote a check, it is C with the check number. If the bank or a card did it, it is M with a memorandum number.

Learn

A petty cash fund is set at a fixed amount. When it runs low, you total the slips, count the cash, and write one check to refill it.

Lakeshore Bike Shop's fund is 200.00. Two payment slips: Supplies 48.20 and Advertising 36.00. The cash counted in the box is 115.30.

Total payments ...... 48.20 + 36.00 Recorded balance .... fund − payments Cash short .......... recorded balance − cash counted Replenish ........... payments + short

Tips & tricks

  • Recorded balance = fund amount − total payments. It is what should be in the box.
  • Short = recorded balance − cash actually counted. If the box has less than it should, it is short.
  • Replenish check = total payments + short. This brings the box back up to the full fund amount.
  • Quick check: cash counted + replenish check = fund amount. If not, you made a mistake.
  • Slip amounts go in the column for the account they were spent on: Supplies, Advertising Expense, Miscellaneous Expense.

Learn

Replenishing the fund: you write Check No. 330 for 84.70 (48.20 + 36.00 + 0.50). Sort the accounts into the entry. One response does not belong.

Tips & tricks

  • Petty Cash is NOT in the replenish entry. The fund stays at 200.00. Only Cash goes down.
  • One credit, several debits. Credit Cash once for the whole check. Debit each expense account for its slip.
  • Cash Short is a debit to Cash Short and Over, like an expense.
  • Check that it balances: debits add to 84.70, credits add to 84.70.
